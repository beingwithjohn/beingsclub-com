Before you begin

This is a map, not a method.

This map won't give you a single specific meditation technique. There are enough voices doing that. These pages describe territory — what the body, the heart and the mind actually are as places to rest attention — so that you can find your own way.

Nothing here asks you to believe anything. That is deliberate. Every claim on these pages is written to be tested against your own experience, and to be let go of if it doesn't land.

The premise is simple: you already have what these practices point at. Presence, resonance, spaciousness — none of it is manufactured from nought. It is uncovered. Practice is only sometimes cultivation and often just the removing of what obscures.

A rhythm — effort and rest — runs through everything here. We build a habit of attention, then let the habit show us what needs no habit at all.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to change. Nothing to do.

The how

How to explore the map

Before the where, a word on the how.
However you practice, these two qualities are worth bringing with you.

Quality one
Gentleness

A softening. A way of treating ourselves and others as precious, and a counter to the harshness that can creep in when we apply effort. Without it, practice becomes another place where we're hard on ourselves.

Quality two
Precision

An exactness; a way of meeting reality with complete contact, and a counter to the dullness that can happen when we grow complacent. Without it, practice becomes an unhelpful nap.

Too much gentleness goes slack. Too much precision becomes rigid. The practice lives in both at once — and when they come into balance, a third thing arrives on its own.

What follows
Letting go

Not a technique but a result: right effort, the absence of aggression, the natural release of clinging. It happens naturally, once gentleness and precision are in tune.

The one practicality

A suggested posture

Posture is the same balance in physical form: precision with gentleness.
Wakeful and at ease, at the same time. Something to play with until it feels right.

Place
Indoors is good. Outdoors can be better — a garden, a doorway, a park bench, or simply the window open. Weather, birdsong and traffic are not interruptions to the practice; they can be part of practice.
Seat
Chair, cushion, bench or floor — all of them work. Aim to have your hips a little higher than your knees, so the lower back keeps its natural curve instead of collapsing.
Spine
Upright without strain. Think of lengthening from the crown of the head. Uprightness here is dignity, not rigidity.
Hands
Resting palms down on the thighs or cradled in the lap.
Face
Relaxed muscles of the face, mouth closed or lips just separated, tongue soft, chin perhaps very slightly tucked. Breathe through the nose if you can.
Eyes
Open with a soft, unfocused gaze a metre or so ahead, or closed. Try both over a stretch of days; they produce noticeably different practices.
Pain
Move. Look after your body. Enduring real pain teaches nothing except that practice hurts. Lie down, stand, walk. Do what you have to do.

None of this is a prerequisite. Posture is an instrument you have to support your practice — worth tuning and observing the effects of.

The shape of it

Three aspects of experience,
one rhythm

This map describes three places you already have access to but perhaps rarely visit on purpose. Each one has two ways of approach. First you might cultivate — a deliberate practice, something to do. Then you might rest — stop efforting, and let what is already here reveal itself spontaneously.

They are not stages, and you don't graduate from one to the next. Body, heart and mind are all available on any given day; which one you begin with is a matter of what the day is like.

The body is here — unmistakably. The heart resonates outwards and inwards. The mind can open to include everything.

The shape of it05
Territory one

The Body

The body is always in contact with the present. It is a field of changing sensation with its own non-conceptual intelligence — grounded, immediate, already here.

But we rarely stay with it. We fix our attention elsewhere, often to avoid what feels uncomfortable, and lose awareness of that which is never anywhere but now.

To come back is to rediscover that the body is a world in its own right. It has been here for your whole life, a witness to most of every day. Air that was outside a moment ago is inside you now; warmth, light and sound arrive uninvited. The body is sensitive and learning, and that intelligence can be offered to the situations of our lives.

Cultivate
Turn toward sensation

Body scans and walking. Move attention through the body, deepening your capacity to be with the pleasant, the unpleasant and the neutral alike.

Rest
Let awareness diffuse

Stop moving attention. Let the body's natural sensitivity become a ground you relax into, and trust its intelligence to hold your life.

The Body06
What is revealed

Presence.

You don't have to do being here. You already are. After turning toward sensation, dropping all effort, what's left is simply this: embodiment without striving. A trust that the body can be rested in, exactly as it is.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to change. Nothing to do.

The Body · presence07
Territory two

The Heart

The heart is an organ of attunement. It resonates — with our own experience, with others, with the wider world — and it carries its own knowing: a reservoir of care, compassion and courage.

Its capacity to feel is endlessly deep, and because it is so deep, we often find our hearts closing to protect us from the fullness of it.

Opening the heart is not done by force. But we can plant seeds of intention that let it remember its own strength — its courage to face all kinds of pain.

Cultivate
Wish well, on purpose

Loving-kindness. Deliberate warmth, turned toward yourself and then outward, until gentleness begins to feel natural rather than performed.

Rest
Let the heart resonate

Begin in the body, then bring awareness to the heart and let its qualities respond on their own, without needing them to any particular way.

The Heart08
What is revealed

Resonance.

You are not generating the care. You are discovering that the heart does this on its own — it resonates and responds, if you let it. What surfaces is a wide, expansive sense of feeling experience, carried by love and a real sense of capacity and courage.

Notice where the heart's resonance reaches: towards others and the world without excluding oneself. Warmth reaches as readily to an animal, a tree or a place as to a person. It's as natural as ones care for a new-born child.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to change. Nothing to do.

The Heart · resonance09
Territory three

The Mind

The mind thinks — that is what it does. Usually we don't notice we're thinking; we're simply lost to it.

Our culture teaches us to meet a complex world through concept and abstraction, and that never quite satisfies, because the world is not a concept. It is an unfathomable, changing reality.

We make a little more ease for ourselves by loosening the grip of thought as the only way we relate to our lives and noticing the space in which all thoughts arise.

Cultivate
Note thinking as thinking

When you notice you've been thinking, note it, and return. Thought becomes one phenomenon among many — not reality itself.

Rest
Open into space

Through body, then heart, then a recognition of the mind as naturally spacious. Let phenomena arise and pass by themselves.

The Mind10
What is revealed

Spaciousness.

The noticing was always happening in something already open. Thoughts arise and pass without your control; you don't have to run the show.

What remains is a sense of spaciousness, and rest. There is space.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to change. Nothing to do.

The Mind · spaciousness11
The point of a map

Making it your own

A map is no use if you only ever read it. The three aspects of experience — grounding into the body, resonating through the heart, resting in the spaciousness of mind — are yours to explore in whatever order and rhythm your practice asks for.

That freedom is what play means here. Let your practice become a friend.

If overwhelmed
Begin with the body
If angry
Begin with the heart
If scattered
Begin with the mind

Twenty minutes is standard, and sitting daily matters far more than sitting long. Experiment. On a hard day, five minutes counts. When you lapse — everyone lapses — simply begin again. Beginning again is not the failure. It is the practice.

And remember: what you were reaching for was never produced by any of this. Presence, resonance, spaciousness — they are here all the time.

We build a habit only to find what needs no habit at all.
