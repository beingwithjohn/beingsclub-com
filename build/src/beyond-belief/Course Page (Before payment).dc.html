What you're agreeing to

A commitment, not a course.

Almost everything that makes meditation worth doing happens on the unremarkable days between classes. Almost nobody sustains those days alone. So we don't.

Ten of us begin on the same Wednesday, sit through the same thirty-five days, and come back each week to explore whatever actually happened — including the days it didn't. That shared calendar is the container.

“Every day you can” is meant literally, and it is not a test. Some days it will be twenty minutes on your meditation seat. Some days it will be twenty minutes in a parked car, and that still counts. We're not building a streak — we're building thirty-five days of continuous, shared intention that you can feel the shape of by the end.

You will also be sitting through a real turn in the year. We begin in the week before the equinox, when light and dark are level, and finish in late October with the evenings visibly shorter. Thirty-five days is long enough to experience the waves of change that accompany a regular practice — a better teacher of impermanence than anything I could say about it.

Why it's built this way

It's not always easy to be curious.

Sometimes it's easier to defend your point of view, deny something as possible, or simply cast another off as dumb, or stupid, or weird.

It takes more courage to admit you may not know the truth of what is happening — to open to possibilities beyond what you'd previously thought possible. It takes courage to admit you were wrong. And on the other side of such acts of courage is a kind of liberation: you get freed from who you thought you were, and from the certainty of what you thought must be.

That open space doesn't deliver you from the earthly challenges that need navigating. But consciously recognising the openness of how things can unfold makes your approach to those challenges lighter, more optimistic, and often more effective.

We are all capable of being open-to-possibility creatures. It's only since birth that we've accumulated the beliefs that limit what we think is possible — and they are not who we inherently are, nor what the world inherently is.

The manifestation world offers techniques that clumsily hack this natural openness, asking you to effortfully stretch your self-concept past its old limits through affirmation. I've found that can work, to a degree. But affirmations are made to be certainties, and if you're anything like me they often feel a little disingenuous even when they work.

I think that's because affirmational work suffers from the unsteadiness of unearned certainty.

The more sustainable and enjoyable path is to recognise the natural, uncontrived, simple existence of spacious openness itself — to see that all your thoughts are already arising through a particular way of seeing, built by habit and conditioning, rather than being grounded in the unchallengeable truth of the open now. Making contact with the ground, resonating with what is true, and opening to what is then arising provides the raw materials to become the artist of your own life.

That is what these thirty-five days are for. Not a better set of beliefs.

The wide expanse of now: with no answers, and a great deal of wonder.

Before you decide

Fair questions

I've never meditated. Will I be behind?

No. This is built for people starting from nothing. Nobody is asked to perform, and nobody sits for longer than a beginner comfortably can.

How long do I have to sit each day?

Twenty-minutes is standard. However, sitting daily matters far more than sitting long. Five minutes on a hard day is a real sit, not a failure.

What happens when I miss days?

Everyone does sometimes — let it be part of the learning if it happens in the thirty-five days, rather than treated as a lapse. Beginning again is the practice.

What if I have to miss a Wednesday?

Tell me and you'll get what you need to stay with the week — the practice, and where we got to. That week's partner can fill in the rest. Missing one is survivable; missing three isn't.

Do I need a cushion, an app, anything?

A chair and somewhere you won't be interrupted. There's a simple daily log — an email, one tap to say you sat — but it's optional, takes seconds, and keeps no streaks.

Can I sit outdoors?

Please do — a garden, a park, a doorway, or just the window open. Cold, birdsong and traffic are not interruptions to the practice; they are what you are practising with. Wrap up, though. Late October can be cold – at least it is in the UK.

Is this religious?

No. The practices come out of contemplative traditions and I won't pretend otherwise, but nothing is asked of you as belief. Bring your own metaphysics, or none.

What if it isn't worth it?

The £222 is paid up front — but if you sit the thirty-five days and it wasn't worth it, tell me and I'll return it. The experiment extends to the price.

What's shared in the group stays there?

Yes. Ten people, nothing recorded, nothing repeated outside the room. That's the condition that makes it possible to say the true thing.
