Sits · learn to meditate, in company

A practice made yours, in company.

A Sit is a small group learning to meditate together over a fixed stretch of days. We all sit every day we can, and meet one evening a week.

Each meeting is another chance to explore. The days that follow are where it becomes yours — with a practice partner alongside you.

No experience needed. Every instruction is offered as an experiment to test in your own experience.

Nothing to prepare, and nothing to believe.

How a week
unfolds

An evening together, then the rest of the week on your own. The same shape repeats until the closing.

01

The evening together

We share how the week has actually gone, perhaps a new principle is introduced, and we are together for 75 minutes.

02

Your own days

You sit every day you can, wherever you can — twenty minutes is standard. One principle, returned to.

03

A practice partner

Someone to share the practice with each week. However the two of you decide to stay in touch — paired as companions, not monitors.

04

One tap to say you sat

A short email at a time you choose. You see who else has sat that day. You record the journey of your own practice.

Meditation practice is super-ordinary.

What you're agreeing to

A commitment, not a course.

Almost everything that makes meditation worth doing happens through the gradual accumulation of practice.

So a small group begins on the same day, sits the same stretch of days, and comes back each week to explore what actually happened — including the days it didn't happen. A shared calendar is the container.

“Every day you can” is meant literally, and it isn't a test. Some days it's twenty minutes on a cushion; some days it's twenty minutes in a parked car, and that counts.

Every Sit runs through a real turn in the year, start to finish. Long enough to feel the undercurrent of life's changes while you practise.

Group size

Twelve or less, so John remains available to be in contact with each of you.

Why it's built this way

It isn't always easy to be curious.

It's often easier to defend a point of view than to admit you may not know what's happening. It takes courage to open to possibilities beyond what you thought possible — and on the other side of that courage is a kind of liberation.

Manifestation techniques hack that openness by asking you to affirm your way past your own limits. It can work. But affirmations are made to be certainties, and they often feel disingenuous even when they work.

Affirmational work suffers from the unsteadiness of unearned certainty.

The more sustainable path is to notice the simple field of possibility that's already here — to see that that which is true now rather than what we are hoping or fearing may be true.

That's what Sits are for. For meeting what is true for you now. Not a better set of beliefs.

Belief

Nothing has to be believed.

Every practice is an experiment. Keep what holds up in your own experience.

In recognising openness, there is more room and more freedom.

Who it's for

Sceptics welcome.

This is for you if you've never meditated, or you've tried an app and it never stuck. If you want a practice that survives contact with an ordinary, busy life.

Importantly, Sits are for people who are ready to commit to a sustained period of regular practice as an experiment, with their full curiosity, and earnest engagement.

Probably not for you if

You want a quick fix, or a technique that makes difficult feelings go away.

You're looking for a lineage, a certification, or a final answer.

You want the classes but not the days in between.

These are the wrong weeks for you. Wait for the next Sit — it'll arrive.

Frequently asked questions

I've never meditated. Will I be behind?

No. Sits are purposely designed to accommodate people with no meditation experience. Arguably, everyone should arrive with the beginner's approach.

How long each day?

Twenty minutes is standard and you're encouraged to make time for it. However, sitting daily matters far more than sitting long.

What if I miss days?

Everyone does. Let it be part of the learning rather than a lapse — beginning again is the practice.

What if I miss a class?

Tell me and I'll get you up to speed as best as I can.
Missing one class is survivable, missing three isn't.

Do I need a cushion or an app?

You'll need somewhere you can join meetings from where won't be interrupted. As for meditation, you can meditate on a cushion, stool, or chair. Mostly anywhere works — cold, birdsong and traffic can be part of practice.

Is it recorded?

No. Nothing is recorded, and nothing said in the room is repeated outside it.

Is this religious?

No. The practices come out of contemplative traditions and I won't pretend otherwise, but nothing is asked of you as belief.

What if it isn't worth it?

How is this different from a Salon?

A Salon is one evening a month, mostly about connecting with each other. A Sit goes deeper into practice with a small group over weeks.

Sits are for people who are curious about meditation and ready to commit to giving the practice a real shot.
