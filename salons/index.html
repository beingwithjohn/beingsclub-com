Salons

Space to connect — to oneself, to each other, and to what is possible.

Every Salon begins with meditation. After that it is a room of people talking — about whatever is actually alive for them, with the kind of attention that is hard to find anywhere else.

It runs on the last Sunday of every month, online, and we're together for about two hours. You can come once, or keep coming. Both are normal.

Everyone takes part: cameras on, and nothing is recorded.

Nothing to prepare, nothing to have figured out.

01

Individual practice, held in a shared field. Guided, so nothing is required of you but to be there. If you have never meditated, this is a good place to find out what it is.

02

We talk in pairs, then threes

Randomly assorted — pairs first, then triads. No topic and nothing to get through; the only instruction is to stay curious. What's important makes itself known.

03

We close together

Everyone comes back into one room to land, and to hear what came up elsewhere. It ends when it says it will.

Come as you are and see what happens.

The principle

Curiosity connects.

This is the principle a Salon runs on. We define curiosity as “an orientation to experience that is open to discovery”.

Practised in this way, we create the conditions for the discovery of connection — to ourselves, to each other, and to what is possible. It is also what lets people disagree here without it becoming a fight, and say difficult true things without being managed.

Which is why a Salon asks you to take part. Cameras are on and everybody talks — it is a social gathering, not a broadcast, a webinar or a room of black squares. You don't need experience or anything clever to say, but you do need to be in it as a person.

It is a social gathering, not a therapy group and not a workshop. Nobody is being fixed.

Fair questions

I've never meditated. Is that a problem?

No. The sit is guided, and plenty of people arrive having never done it. Nobody can tell how it went for you, and nobody asks.

Do I have to talk?

Yes — it's a social gathering, and the conversation is the point. Cameras are on and everyone takes part. You don't have to be interesting or arrive with anything worked out; you just have to actually be there.

Is it recorded?

Never. Nothing is recorded and nothing is written up. What's said in the room stays in the room — which is part of why people can be honest in it.

Is it religious?

No. Practices are offered to experiment with and discover the reality of for yourself. Nothing has to be believed, and nothing is asked of you afterwards.

What if I can only come sometimes?

That is how most people come. It is a monthly room, not a course — turn up when it works and leave a gap when it doesn't.

How is this different from a Sit?

A Salon is one evening, monthly, and mostly about meeting each other. A Sit is a longer commitment for going deeper into practice, with a small group over weeks.
