Before you begin

This is a map, not a method.

It won't tell you how to meditate, step by step. There are enough voices doing that, and most of them are trying to get you somewhere. This is quieter. It describes the territory — what the body, the heart and the mind actually are as places to rest attention — so that you can find your own way through them.

Nothing here asks you to believe anything. That is deliberate. Every claim on this page is written to be tested against your own experience, and to be discarded if it doesn't hold.

The premise is simple: you already contain what these practices point at. Presence, warmth, spaciousness — none of it is manufactured by effort. It is uncovered. Practice is only ever the removing of what obscures it.

So we work, and then we discover we didn't have to. That rhythm — effort and rest — runs through everything here. We build a habit of attention, then let the habit show us what needs no habit at all.

Nothing to fix. Nothing to change. Nothing to do.

The how

How to walk the map

Before the where, a word on the how. However you practise, two qualities keep the walking true — and they are less a list than a balance.

Quality one

Gentleness

A softening. A way of treating ourselves and others as precious, and a counter to the harshness that creeps in whenever we apply effort. Without it, practice becomes another place to be hard on yourself.

Quality two

Precision

Its opposite and its partner. An exactness; a way of meeting reality with complete contact, and a counter to the dullness that settles in when we grow complacent. Without it, practice becomes a nap.

Too much gentleness goes slack. Too much precision goes hard. The practice lives in holding both at once — and when they come into balance, a third quality arrives on its own.

What follows
Letting go

Not a technique but a result: right effort, the absence of aggression, the natural release of clinging. You don't do it. It happens, once gentleness and precision are in tune.

The arc

Five principles, five doors

Each Wednesday takes one principle — a way of holding the practice. The three territories are not divided between the weeks: body, heart and mind are all here from the first day, and you keep returning to them throughout.

Between the Wednesdays, we sit — every day we can, all of us. The Wednesdays are when we meet. The sit runs for the whole thirty-five days.

Each week

You'll be paired with a different practice partner each week — someone sitting the same days as you, holding the same principle, to sit alongside and speak plainly with for those seven days. Over the five weeks you'll practise with five different people. The inner life was never meant to be walked alone.

The one practicality

How you sit

Posture is the same balance in physical form: precision in the spine, gentleness everywhere else. Alert and at ease, at the same time. That's the whole instruction. The rest is detail.

Seat

Chair, cushion, bench or floor — all of them work. Aim to have your hips a little higher than your knees, so the lower back keeps its natural curve instead of collapsing.

Spine

Upright without strain. Think of lengthening from the crown of the head rather than pulling the shoulders back. Uprightness here is dignity, not rigidity.

Hands

Resting on the thighs or held in the lap. Somewhere they can be forgotten about.

Face

Jaw loose, tongue soft, chin very slightly tucked. Breathe through the nose if you can.

Eyes

Open with a soft, unfocused gaze a metre or so ahead, or closed. Try both across the thirty-five days; they produce noticeably different practices.

Pain

Move. Enduring real pain teaches nothing except that practice hurts. Lying down counts. Standing counts. Walking counts.

None of this is a prerequisite. You can begin badly, in a bad chair, on a bad day. Posture is simply the first instrument you have — worth tuning, not worth waiting for.

The shape of it

Three territories, one rhythm

The map moves through three places you already live but rarely visit on purpose. In each one the rhythm is the same. First you cultivate — a deliberate practice, something to do. Then you rest — you stop working, and let the territory show you what was there all along.

The territories widen as you go. The body is intimate — yours alone. The heart reaches toward others. The mind opens to include everything.

Territory one Practised every week

The Body

The body is always in contact with the present. It is a field of changing sensation with its own non-conceptual intelligence — grounded, immediate, already here.

But we rarely stay with it. We fix our attention elsewhere, often to avoid what feels uncomfortable, and lose contact with the one part of us that is never anywhere but now.

To come back is to rediscover that the body is a world in its own right — and that its intelligence can be offered to the situations of our lives.

Cultivate

Turn toward sensation

Body scans and walking. Move attention through the body, deepening your capacity to be with the pleasant, the unpleasant and the neutral alike.

Rest

Let awareness diffuse

Stop moving attention. Let the body's natural sensitivity become a ground you relax into, and trust its intelligence to hold your life.

What is revealed

Presence.

You don't have to do being here. You already are. After a while turning toward sensation, the effort drops, and what's left is simply this: embodiment without striving. A trust that the body can be rested in, exactly as it is.

This is the first taste of the whole thirty-five days in miniature. You worked, and then the working became unnecessary.

Territory two Practised every week

The Heart

The heart is an organ of attunement. It resonates — with our own experience, with others, with the wider world — and it carries its own knowing: a reservoir of care, compassion and courage.

Its capacity to feel is endlessly deep, and because it is so deep, we often find our hearts closing to protect us from the fullness of it.

Opening the heart is not done by force. But we can plant seeds of intention that let it remember its own strength — its courage to face all kinds of pain.

Cultivate

Wish well, on purpose

Loving-kindness. Deliberate warmth, turned toward yourself and then outward, until gentleness begins to feel natural rather than performed.

Rest

Let the heart resonate

Begin in the body, then bring awareness to the heart and let its qualities respond on their own, without recruiting them.

What is revealed

Resonance.

You are not generating the care. You are discovering that the heart does this on its own — it responds, if you let it. What surfaces is a wide, expansive feeling of love, carried by a real sense of capacity and courage.

And notice where the heart's resonance reaches: toward others. This is why we don't practise alone, and why you have a partner for these five weeks.

Territory three Practised every week

The Mind

The mind thinks — that is what it does. Usually we don't notice we're thinking; we're simply lost in it.

Our culture teaches us to meet a complex world through concept and abstraction, and that never quite satisfies, because the world is not a concept. It is an unfathomable, changing reality.

We make a little more ease for ourselves by loosening the grip of thought as the only way we relate to our lives.

Cultivate

Note thinking as thinking

When you notice you've been thinking, note it, and return. Thought becomes one phenomenon among many — not reality itself.

Rest

Open into space

Through body, then heart, then a recognition of the mind as naturally spacious. Let phenomena arise and pass by themselves.

What is revealed

Spaciousness.

The noticing was always happening in something already open. Thoughts arise and pass without your control; you don't have to run the show.

There is space — and it is there when you need it. What remains is a sense of spaciousness, and rest.

The point of a map Weeks four and five

Making it your own

A map is no use if you only ever read it. The three territories — grounding into the body, resonating through the heart, resting in the spaciousness of mind — are yours to walk in whatever order and rhythm your life allows.

That freedom is what play means here. Not that it doesn't matter — that it can't be got wrong.

If agitated
Begin with the body
If closed
Begin with the heart
If overwhelmed
Don't cultivate. Rest.

Experiment. Cultivate the habits that reveal each natural quality, and let go of those that obscure them. On a hard day, five minutes counts. When you lapse — everyone lapses — simply begin again. Beginning again is not the failure. It is the practice.

And what you were reaching for was never produced by any of this. Presence, resonance, spaciousness — the practice only stops obscuring them. They were here the whole time.

We build a habit only to find what needs no habit at all.
